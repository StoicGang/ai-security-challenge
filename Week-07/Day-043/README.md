## Learning Log
---
### The Challenge
<!-- What problem were you actually trying to solve today, in one sentence? -->
Today's challenge was to identify who owns the risk and what should be implemented for my AI project.

### Key Concept
<!-- The idea in plain English — no jargon, as if explaining to a junior. -->
Governs means deciding who is responsible for the AI systems risk and what system and organization must follow in order to identify the risk and manage the risk.

### How I Approached It
<!-- Numbered steps, briefly. This is the only "steps" section — everything else below should be about the mechanism, not the process. -->
1. Read the NIST Govern overview.
2. Reasoned about risk ownership and the difference between policy and technical controls.
3. Applied those ideas to your Week 3 project.

### Findings
- Owner: I am accountable for the AI risk because I am the sole developer and decision-maker.
- Policy: The AI should use only authorized or retrieved information.
- Enforcement/control: Restrict the AI's access to only the necessary files or retrieved context.
- Security effect: This limits the impact of an attacker manipulating the AI into accessing unrelated information.

### Key Learning
<!-- The one thing you understand now that you didn't before today. -->
I learned that owning AI risk is not only about developing the AI system; it is about being accountable for the risks and decisions associated with how the system is used. I also learned to distinguish between a governance policy and the technical controls used to enforce that policy.

### Ah-ha Moment
<!-- The specific instant it clicked, and what made it click. -->
It clicked when I understood that I was a sole developer, so it does not mean that the AI risk remains unnoticed and unowned. As a sole developer, the responsibility increases to manage all the risk management principles of the project.

### What I'd Do Differently
<!-- If you redid today, what would you change? -->
I would create a reusable set of questions to identify AI risk ownership, applicable policies, and controls before starting future projects. This would help me systematically distinguish governance decisions from technical implementation instead of discovering the distinction during the project.

### Residual Questions
<!-- What still doesn't make sense? Carry this into tomorrow or the Day 6 buffer. -->
I still want a reusable set of questions that helps me identify the AI-risk owner, applicable policies, risks, and technical controls consistently across future projects.
