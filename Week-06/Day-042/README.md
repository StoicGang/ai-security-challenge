## Learning Log
---

### The Challenge
<!-- What problem were you actually trying to solve today, in one sentence? -->
Identify the basic difference between AI governance and AI security, particularly how organizational policies differ from technical security controls.

### Key Concept
<!-- The idea in plain English — no jargon, as if explaining to a junior. -->
AI governance is implemented across the organization through policies, responsibilities, and rules that guide how AI should be used. AI security focuses on protecting the AI system, its data, tools, and operations through technical security controls.

### Key Learning
<!-- The one thing you understand now that you didn't before today. -->
AI governance defines organizational policies, responsibilities, and acceptable AI use, while AI security focuses on protecting AI systems, data, tools, and operations through security controls.

### Governance vs Security
<!-- The specific instant it clicked, and what made it click. -->
1. Is the statement defining what the organization, employees, or stakeholders are allowed or required to do with AI?
→ AI Governance
2. Is the statement describing a technical control that protects the AI system, data, tools, or operations from unauthorized or harmful actions?
→ AI Security
3. Does the statement contain both a rule and a technical enforcement mechanism?
→ Both, and separate the governance requirement from the security control.
4. Ask: "Is this telling me what should be allowed, or what technically prevents something from happening?"
→ Should be allowed → Governance
→ Technically prevents → Security

### What I'd Do Differently
<!-- If you redid today, what would you change? -->
Use a simple classification framework earlier to distinguish governance requirements from technical security controls instead of treating both as general AI risk controls.

### Residual Questions
<!-- What still doesn't make sense? Carry this into tomorrow or the Day 6 buffer. -->
How do governance requirements translate into specific technical security controls, and where does the responsibility shift between governance and security teams?
